Overtime at your day job

The highest hourly rate available to you, and the only one with no startup cost, no customers and no tax paperwork. At time-and-a-half your hour is worth $45 — more than almost any side hustle pays after you subtract the hours it really takes.

Check whether your employer actually offers it, and whether hours are capped. Taxed as normal wages, so it keeps about eight points more than the two streams below — your employer still pays half your Social Security.

Resale — the thing you've already proven

You bought a beanie at $275 and can sell it near $450. That's a real skill and most people don't have it. The limit isn't listing, it's sourcing: finding items that actually carry that margin takes the time. Budget about two hours per item all in — hunting, photos, listing, packing, shipping.

Each item ties up cash until it sells. At ~$275 a piece, $1,375 of your money is sitting in inventory.

Contract work through your business account

You already have a business checking account, which means you've done the annoying part. Whatever skill your day job pays $30 an hour for, the same work billed directly usually goes for $52. Weekends and evenings, invoiced, not hourly-waged.

You pay both halves of Social Security on this — that's why the set-aside above is higher than your W-2 tax rate. Put it in a separate account the day you're paid.

The portfolio itself — the one that costs no hours

A total-market index fund pays out roughly 1.3% a year in dividends on top of its growth. Today that's pocket change. That's the point: it starts tiny and ends up larger than everything above it, and it never asks you to work a Saturday. The three streams above exist to make this one bigger, faster.

Notice what the numbers say

All three time-based streams land within a few dollars an hour of each other — roughly $34–45 an hour after tax. Your labour is worth about the same wherever you sell it, so the honest question isn't "which hustle" but how many extra hours you're willing to sell. Right now that's 39 hours a month on top of your 173, which is a 49-hour week.

This is also why the flashy options are usually worse. Anything promising far more per hour is either taking a risk you can't see or isn't paying what it claims.

Do it in this order

Three accounts, then stop thinking about it

No 401(k) at your job, so the Roth is your best tax shelter and it's yours to open — nobody has to offer it to you. Set each transfer to fire automatically on payday.

  1. Cash cushion — three months of bills

    High-yield savings, separate bank from checking, no debit card. This is what stops a slow month or a car repair from turning into credit card debt. Fill it once and leave it alone — it's insurance, not an investment.

  2. Roth IRA — max it every single year

    Open it yourself at Fidelity, Schwab or Vanguard in about fifteen minutes. Money goes in after tax and every dollar it grows into comes out tax-free at retirement. Given no 401(k), this is the best account available to you and the limit doesn't roll over — a year you skip is gone permanently. Opening it is not investing. Buy an index fund inside it or the money just sits there as cash.

  3. Regular brokerage for everything above the limit

    Same brokerage, ordinary taxable account, no cap on contributions. One total-market index fund is already a complete portfolio — VTI, VOO, FXAIX or SWTSX all do the same job for nearly no fee. Automate the buy every payday and then don't look at it.

A note on the business account

Once contract work is regular, ask an accountant about a Solo 401(k) or SEP-IRA. Both let a self-employed person shelter far more per year than a Roth IRA allows — tens of thousands rather than a few. You don't need it at $624 a month, but it's the reason growing the contract stream is worth more than it looks.

Two things this page assumes

7% a year is a historical average, not a promise. Roughly what a broad US index fund has returned above inflation over the long run, so these figures are in today's buying power. Real years are lumpy — some are −20%. The average only shows up for people who stay in through the bad ones.

Check this year's Roth IRA limit before you set the transfer. The page uses $7,000; the IRS adjusts it for inflation, so confirm the current number.
